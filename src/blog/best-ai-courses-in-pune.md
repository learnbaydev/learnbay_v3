  glossary:
    - term: LLM (Large Language Model)
      definition:
        A model trained on large text data that can generate and understand language, such as
        GPT-4o, Claude or LLaMA.
    - term: RAG (Retrieval-Augmented Generation)
      definition:
        A method where an LLM answers using relevant documents fetched at query time.
        Production-grade RAG adds re-ranking, evaluation and failure handling.
    - term: Fine-tuning
      definition:
        Training a pre-trained model further on your own data. LoRA and QLoRA are low-cost
        fine-tuning methods.
    - term: Agentic AI
      definition:
        AI systems that plan steps, call tools and act on a goal with limited human input.
    - term: Multi-agent frameworks
      definition:
        Tools such as LangGraph, CrewAI and AutoGen that coordinate several agents.
    - term: MCP (Model Context Protocol)
      definition: An open standard that connects AI models to tools and data sources.
    - term: A2A (Agent2Agent protocol)
      definition:
        An open standard that lets agents from different systems talk to each other.
    - term: Vector database
      definition:
        A database that stores embeddings for similarity search, such as Pinecone, FAISS or Chroma.
    - term: LLMOps
      definition:
        Practices for deploying, monitoring, versioning and controlling the cost of LLM
        applications.
    - term: MLOps
      definition: Practices for deploying and maintaining classical ML models in production.
    - term: Guardrails
      definition:
        Checks that keep AI output safe, accurate and compliant. Essential in BFSI and healthcare.
    - term: Small language model (SLM)
      definition: A smaller, task-specific model that is cheaper to run than a general LLM.
    - term: Forward Deployed Engineer
      definition:
        An engineer who builds and integrates AI systems inside a client’s environment.

### The 6-Phase Roadmap to Switch Into AI

**Phase 1: Assess and pick a target role (weeks 1 to 2).** List your current skills and domain. Pick one AI role from the table above that sits closest to your work.

**Phase 2: Build foundations (months 1 to 3).** Learn Python, basic statistics and SQL. Add ML fundamentals such as regression, classification and evaluation metrics. Non-coders focus on AI tools and prompting instead.

**Phase 3: Learn Generative AI (months 3 to 5).** Cover LLMs, prompt engineering, embeddings, vector databases, RAG and fine-tuning with LoRA.

**Phase 4: Add agents and production skills (months 5 to 7).** Build agents with LangGraph, CrewAI or AutoGen, connect tools through MCP, and learn LLMOps, monitoring and guardrails.

**Phase 5: Build a domain portfolio (months 6 to 9).** Ship 3 to 5 deployed projects, with at least one from your current industry.

**Phase 6: Prepare and apply (months 8 to 9).** Update your resume and LinkedIn, practise mock interviews and system design, and apply to GCCs and product firms in Pune.

In short, the path looks like this:

Assess → Foundations → Generative AI → Agents and LLMOps → Domain portfolio → Interviews

### What Makes a Career Switch Work?

**Stay in your domain.** A BFSI analyst moving into BFSI AI changes one variable. Moving to healthcare AI at the same time changes two.

**Learn on a live schedule.** A live, mentored batch keeps you accountable through a 6 to 9 month switch.

**Show proof early.** Share your projects on GitHub and LinkedIn while you are still learning.

**Keep your job while you learn.** Weekend live batches let you switch without a gap in income.
