## Insights Into the Impact of AI on India's Job Market: Is Your Career Ready?

As of mid-2026, AI has not caused massive job losses for many. According to Forbes India reports, unemployment after the AI evolution still sits at 3.1% in the country. On the other hand, AI itself has created impressive career opportunities with over 290,000 new job postings in 2025. However, the impact of AI on IT jobs in India shows a decline in hiring, with FY24 recording only 60,000+ job postings. In short, AI is redefining the workforce and associated operations instead of completely replacing them. Now, in 2026, it's not “AI vs. human”; it's actually “AI vs. professionals who cannot direct AI”.

In 2026, every IT professional, analyst, marketer, or content creator is asking one question – “Will AI replace my job?”

A quick answer is no.

According to reports, over 45.2% of India’s AI usage goes to IT development operations and engineering tasks compared to other uses. However, many professionals are failing to secure their jobs or make relevant decisions due to AI-specific headlines.

This blog will break down the facts on jobs, skills, and career paths that every working professional should know in 2026.

## Impact of AI on the Job Market of India – Sector-Wise Analysis

Even in 2026, there are major tasks in India that human experts do better than large language models (LLMs). Over 43% of the Indian workforce is still involved in agriculture, trade, construction, and manufacturing sectors where chatbots or AI agents cannot generate wins.

AI adoption in India is creating history and bringing productive results to some industries, not for all.

The AI-exposed IT and ITeS workforce is about 5.8 million people, roughly 1% of India’s ~600 million-strong labour force. In short, only 16% of India’s total IT workforce is AI-skilled as of 2026, as stated by NASSCOM.

So, the real fact is that if you’re a tech professional, you may be at risk due to AI, while the rest of the sectors are still safe.

If we compare PwC, Anthropic, and NASSCOM reports, over 62% of large-scale Indian firms use GenAI tools to accomplish their tasks. But skills gaps are there.

Following this, medium-scale enterprises are catching up with AI faster than others, with just a 2.4% skill gap. However, small-scale enterprises are showing potential knowledge gaps in AI.

## The Impact of the AI Paradox: Rising AI Hiring vs. Rising Tech Layoffs

This is the context that is actually buzzing across India. AI adoption in India has cut the workforce into two segments – one that sees growing hiring trends and the other that is facing massive layoffs.

### The Tech Layoff Side

NDTV's labour report states India’s 2nd worst hit comes with the tech layoffs in the 1st half of 2026 accounting for roughly 7.16% of the 1.28 lakh global tech jobs.
NITI Aayog’s roadmap for job creation in the AI economy 2025 report reflects a worst-case scenario where the tech workforce may be reduced to 6 million by 2031 due to AI dominance.
As AI takes supremacy over IT companies in India, more than 35K jobs are at risk of layoffs in the upcoming months of 2026, as stated by an ET Prime report.
AI dominance is seen across the fintech, software, and automobile sectors as work automation takes place in 2026. – India Today 2026. AI is automating tax, financial tasks, coding, and automobile operations without human intervention.

Indeed, tech layoffs are getting severe, affecting the economic and psychological well-being of professionals. Hence, upskilling has become crucial to survive tech layoffs and the AI’s impact on employment. Professionals can develop 4 essential skills that Big tech firms cannot replace in 2026.

### The Rising Hiring Trend

NASSCOM's Strategic Review 2026 estimates the Indian tech industry added a net 1.35 lakh jobs in FY2026. This reflects an aggressive hiring trend to optimize work productivity in the long run.
AI-led hiring rates are increasing, as Naukri reports over 82,000 AI job postings in July 2026.
PIB reports on AI hiring specifications state that 6 million professionals have already been hired in the tech and AI ecosystem, and it is expected to increase India’s economy by $1.7 trillion in revenue by 2035 through AI-driven operations.
Industry reports state that over 89% of total startups in India have already adopted AI for core operations.

However, AI literacy in India is still a concern for many professionals and career roles. Various big firms in India report that Generative AI adoption across functional teams has increased their productivity gains.

Skills matter more than educational degrees, as around 73% of employers demand to hire AI-skilled experts rather than generic ones.

Indeed, AI layoffs are increasing, and AI hiring is simultaneously pacing up in India. And the main reason is that many companies are aggressively restructuring their workforce for AI readiness. Professionals in routine roles and resistant to AI literacy are losing their jobs to highly-skilled AI experts who can build, manage, and deploy AI models.

The clear and concise answer to this query is: IT professionals.

The fact does not indicate a growing unemployment trend in India; instead, it shows that professionals are not getting hired for trending roles. IT professionals in Bangalore, Hyderabad, Pune, and Delhi are highly exposed to AI-related roles.

## The Other Side of the Story: Will AI Replace Your Job?

AI replacing your job is real – but not the complete story.

Professionals in the tech sector fear AI dominance as it has started doing core technical tasks, such as coding, debugging, etc. However, you must note that work augmentation has now taken over work automation.

That means AI agents are now assisting human experts rather than automating their daily tasks. Hence, professionals with proven AI knowledge can work collaboratively with such agents and models.

According to the PwC Global Job Report 2026, AI is introducing a two-track labor market where professionals should have both AI technical skills and leadership traits to tackle intricacies.

Following a Generative AI learning roadmap can help tech professionals gain essential skills and work collaboratively with AI models for various tasks.

In short, professionals who are ready to upgrade their skills in real time and use AI to enhance their productivity will stay competitive.

In addition, some new roles are coming in 2026, such as AI/ML engineers, AI platform builders, AI product managers, and AI governance specialists. According to the IMF Skills Report 2026, professionals should have at least one specific AI skill to land promising roles in India and overseas.

The World Economic Forum reflects on four typical scenarios that are expected to redefine AI and talent strategies by 2030.

Supercharged progress: AI evolution and dominance in the past few years have made groundbreaking progress, but governance and social safety remain concerns.
AI displacement: It encourages professionals to develop AI-driven skills to stay competitive and secure their jobs, as AI may displace a few major tasks.
Co-Pilot: AI progress and readiness gradually shift focus toward AI augmentation (52%), reshaping business models.
Stalled progress: Despite AI readiness, talent gaps are widely seen across sectors that need to be addressed.

## What does this mean for Indian Professionals?

The data points to a clear conclusion: AI isn't eliminating India's tech workforce; it's redefining & re-sorting it.

Professionals involved in easily-automatable tasks with no supportive AI skills are at higher risk. The professionals who've moved beyond "AI-aware" to "AI-skilled" are seeing strong industry demand. They are now comfortable working with machine learning, GenAI tooling, data pipelines, and increasingly, agentic AI frameworks.

For working professionals and career-switchers watching this shift unfold, 3 things stand out amid AI’s impact on the job market:
Entry-level roles are highly AI-exposed — traditional fresher hiring in IT/BPO is being revamped, so entry-level professionals face the most pressure to differentiate their skills in applied AI and data rather than routine technical skills.
The AI skills gap is unavoidable — with only an estimated 16% of India's IT workforce currently AI-skilled against demand. Hence, structured upskilling has a measurable payoff against AI dominance.
Judgment-based and specialist roles are the safest — ML engineering, applied data science, AI governance, and domain-expert consulting roles are growing even as routine coding roles shrink.
Then, who should worry?
Freshers or entry-level professionals: AI agents are handling essential tasks on behalf of human experts. Hence, freshers with little or no real-time AI knowledge can be at risk.
Professionals with 2–5 years of experience resistant to AI upskilling: Experts who do not use AI tools and are not willing to gain AI literacy may get replaced sooner.
Senior engineers: Such IT professionals are at risk due to AI-generated code. If they fail to upgrade their skills on time, AI may replace them.
Managers: Senior managers with routine tasks, documentation, coordination, and status update responsibilities are at high risk.

No wonder career upskilling is the only solution to survive AI-driven layoffs or industry restructuring.

Professionals who constantly master in-demand tools, gain AI literacy, work on agentic AI projects, and earn certifications can thrive. It empowers professionals to stay ahead of AI developments and shift themselves from the “at-risk” to the “in-demand” range.
